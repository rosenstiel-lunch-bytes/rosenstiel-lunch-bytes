**Speaker**: Michael Fischer

**Title**: From Prompt to Page: Using the Claude Desktop App and Artifacts in Research

**Abstract**: Most people know AI assistants as chat windows, where you ask a question and get text back. The Claude desktop app works differently. It opens your files, writes and runs code, checks its own output, and hands back a finished result. Often that result is an artifact: an interactive web page, document or slide deck, hosted for you, that colleagues can explore and that updates at the same link when you revise it. This talk is for people who have never used Claude as well as those who already do. It covers choosing a model, the “effort” setting that controls how hard the model works on a task, and how artifacts can turn an analysis into something people can explore, teach from, or use to make a decision. Here I will show the utility (or lack thereof) of artifacts built at three effort levels from the same short request to visualize Hurricane Melissa’s (2025) rapid intensification. The results ranged from a sketch drawn from memory in two minutes to a page built on aircraft radar, recon, and satellite data in 46 minutes. The comparison shows what extra effort buys and what it costs. I will close with practical guidance on how to ask and how to check what you get, along with some cautionary tales. No prior experience is needed.
